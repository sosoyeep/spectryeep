# Standards overview

A result is **defensible** when it follows a recognised, documented **test method** and is run by
a competent (often **ISO/IEC 17025-accredited**) lab. This note maps the landscape; the child
notes list specific methods.

> [!warning] **Verify editions.** Standard numbers are reused but content is revised. Always
> confirm the **current published edition** (and any national adoption) before citing one for
> compliance, procurement or certification. See [[Sources and further reading]].

## Who publishes what

- **ASTM International** (USA) — the most-cited spark-OES test methods (E-series). →
  [[ASTM methods for spark OES]]
- **ISO** (international) and **EN** (Europe, often = ISO) — spark-OES and combustion methods. →
  [[ISO and EN methods]]
- **GB / GB/T** (China) — national standards; relevant to Chinese-made instruments like JIEBO. →
  [[Chinese GB-T standards]]
- **JIS** (Japan), **BS/DIN** (national), **SAE/AMS** (aerospace) — product & method specs.

## Two kinds of standard to keep straight

1. **Test-method standards** — *how to perform the analysis* (e.g. ASTM E415 spark-OES of
   carbon/low-alloy steel). These govern your OES procedure, calibration and reporting.
2. **Material/product standards** — *what composition a grade must have* (e.g. ASTM A36,
   EN 10025, AISI/SAE grades, ASTM B series for Cu/Al). These define the **pass/fail limits** you
   compare results against.

## Supporting standards
- **ISO/IEC 17025** — lab competence/accreditation.
- **ISO Guide 30/31/33/35** — reference materials & their use.
- **ISO 5725** — accuracy (trueness & precision) of measurement methods.
- Statistics & MU underpin [[Quality control and SPC]] and
