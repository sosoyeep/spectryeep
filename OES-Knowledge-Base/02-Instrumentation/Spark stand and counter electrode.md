# Spark stand and counter electrode

Where the sample meets the spark. Small, mechanical, and a top source of day-to-day error if
neglected.

## Components

- **Spark stand / spark table** — the chamber the sample seals against; argon flows through it.
- **Sample clamp & spark-stand opening** — the sample must fully **cover and seal the aperture**
  so air can't leak in. Small parts use an **adapter/mask** with a smaller opening.
- **Counter-electrode** — usually **tungsten**, set at a defined **gap** (commonly a few mm)
  from the sample; the discharge jumps this gap.
- **Argon ports** — flush + analysis flow (see [[Argon supply and purity]]).
- Some mobile units use a **jet/probe electrode** for on-site spotting (e.g. [[Surpass F1]]).

## Why it matters

- A poor **seal** lets air in → oxidised burns, bad UV elements → see
  [[Troubleshooting common problems]].
- A dirty/eroded **electrode** or wrong **gap** changes the plasma and biases results.
- **Deposit build-up** on the stand and in the exhaust accumulates conductive metal dust →
  arc-overs and contamination.

## Routine care

- **Clean the electrode** (brush) and **re-set the gap** on the schedule the QC charts demand —
  or whenever control samples drift.
- **Vacuum out the spark-stand dust** regularly; replace the stand window/disc if fitted.
- Keep a stock of **electrodes** and **adapters** (consumables — see
  [[Total cost of ownership and consumables]]).
- Use the correct adapter for **small samples** ([[Small samples, chips and remelting]]).

JIEBO's [[CS2020]] applies the same philosophy to combustion analysers with **automatic
dual-brush cleaning + vacuum dust removal** for 200+ maintenance-free cycles — the principle is
identical: keep the discharge/combustion zone clean and sealed.
