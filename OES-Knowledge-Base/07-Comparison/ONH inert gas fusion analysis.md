# O / N / H — inert-gas-fusion analysis

The standard technique for the **gases dissolved in metals — oxygen, nitrogen and hydrogen** —
which spark OES generally cannot quantify reliably. Co-located with OES and
[[Carbon-sulfur combustion analysis|C/S combustion]] in a full metals lab.

## How it works (inert-gas fusion / melt-extraction)

1. The sample is **fused in a graphite crucible** at very high temperature (up to ~3000 °C)
   under flowing **inert carrier gas** (usually **helium**).
2. Liberated gases are carried to detectors:
   - **Oxygen** → reacts with the graphite to **CO/CO₂** → measured by **NDIR**.
   - **Nitrogen** → measured by **thermal-conductivity (TCD)**.
   - **Hydrogen** → measured by **NDIR or TCD**.
3. Integrated signals give **ppm O, N, H**.

## Why it matters
- **O / N / H control cleanliness, toughness and cracking**: oxide inclusions (O), nitrides &
  aging (N), and **hydrogen embrittlement / flaking** (H) in steels, titanium, nickel and
  reactive metals.
- Trace-level: ppm to sub-ppm sensitivity; matrix-independent.

## Key specs to compare
Ranges (O, N, H each), accuracy/RSD, minimum reading, analysis time (~minutes), furnace power &
max temperature, carrier-gas purity (He ≥99.99 %), balance resolution.

## Standards
[[ASTM methods for spark OES|ASTM E1019]] (steel/Ni/Co), **ASTM E1409** (O,N in Ti),
**ASTM E1447** (H in Ti); GB/T 11261 (O), GB/T 20124 (N) — see [[Chinese GB-T standards]].
