# Carbon–sulfur combustion analysis

The reference technique for **total carbon and sulfur** in metals — complementary to, and often
co-located with, spark OES.

## How it works

1. A weighed sample (often with **W/Sn accelerators**) is burned in a stream of **oxygen** in a
   **high-frequency induction furnace** (or resistance/arc furnace), heating it to combustion.
2. Carbon → **CO₂**, sulfur → **SO₂**.
3. The gases pass through **non-dispersive infrared (NDIR) cells** that absorb at CO₂/SO₂
   wavelengths; absorption ∝ amount → integrate to get **% C** and **% S**.

## Why use it alongside OES

- **Matrix-independent** total C and S — doesn't care about graphite vs carbide form, so it's
  the **reference for carbon in cast iron** where OES needs a chilled sample
  ([[Cast iron and carbon equivalent]]).
- **Very wide range and low limits** — from a few **ppm** to tens of **%** C/S.
- Works on **chips, powders, granules** directly — no flat solid needed
  ([[Small samples, chips and remelting]]).
- Common roles: **arbitration / certification** of C and S, low-S checks for steel cleanliness,
  high-C in cast iron and carbides.

## Key specs to compare
Range (low-C/high-C, low-S/high-S), number of **IR cells** (more cells → wider range without
re-ranging), **precision** (e.g. ≤0.5 % RSD or 1 ppm), analysis time (tens of seconds),
**balance** resolution, and **furnace cleaning** (dust/maintenance).
