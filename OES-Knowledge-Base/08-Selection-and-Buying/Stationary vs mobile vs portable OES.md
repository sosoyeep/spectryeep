# Stationary vs mobile vs portable OES

OES comes in three physical formats. Pick by **where the sample is** and **how good the numbers
must be**.

## The three formats

| | **Stationary / bench** | **Mobile (cart)** | **Portable / handheld** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Where | Fixed lab/deck | Wheeled to the part | Carried to the part |
| Sample | Bring sample to it | Probe brings to part | Probe on the part |
| Performance | **Best** (full vacuum/UV, all elements) | Very good (incl. UV: C,P,S) | Good; light-element-limited |
| Argon | Plumbed supply | On-board cylinder | Small/none |
| Use | Certification, melt control | Large parts, on-site PMI | Field sorting, quick PMI |
| JIEBO | [[Innovate T5]] · [[Noble T7]] · [[Exquis T4]] · [[JB-750]] | [[Surpass F1]] | (handheld → [[F7000 Pro]] LIBS / [[F6000 Pro]] XRF) |

## When to go mobile (e.g. [[Surpass F1]])

- The part is **too big/fixed to cut** (pipelines, pressure vessels, castings, structures).
- You still need **carbon / UV light elements on-site** — true mobile **spark OES** keeps a UV
  optic and argon, unlike most handhelds. Surpass F1: built-in battery + argon, jet electrode,
  UV analysis of C, P, S, B, As, Sn, Se, N.
- Trade-off: somewhat less performance and more handling than a bench unit.

## When portable handheld is enough
Fast **sorting / PMI** where carbon isn't critical (or rough carbon-equivalent via LIBS), no
prep, non-destructive. That's [[OES vs XRF vs LIBS|XRF or LIBS]] territory
([[F6000 Pro]], [[F7000 Pro]]).

## Decision rule
- Best numbers + carbon, sample comes to lab → **stationary**.
- Carbon/UV needed but part can't move → **mobile spark OES**.
- Speed/sorting, no carbon, in the field → **handheld XRF/LIBS**.
