# Sample requirements by matrix

Geometry and handling, on top of the surface rules in [[Surface grinding and milling]].

## General geometry

- **Big enough to seal** the [[Spark stand and counter electrode|spark-stand aperture]] and to
  **act as a heat sink** so the spot doesn't overheat. Too-thin or too-small parts overheat and
  may not seal → use an **adapter** ([[Small samples, chips and remelting]]).
- **Flat face** larger than the aperture, free of curvature at the burn spot.
- **Conductive contact** to the stand (the sample completes the circuit).
- Plan for **multiple burns** on fresh, non-overlapping spots; average them.

## By matrix

- **Carbon / low-alloy steel** — grind; analyse fresh; multiple burns averaged. See
  [[Iron and steel analysis]].
- **Stainless / high-alloy steel** — same, but **homogeneity** matters more; segregation and
  delta-ferrite can scatter results → more burns, [[Single spark evaluation (PDA-SDA)|single- spark evaluation]].
- **Cast iron** — carbon must be measured on a **chilled / white-iron sample** (graphite in grey
  iron sparks non-uniformly); a chilled "wafer"/coin from a thermal-analysis cup or a quenched
  chill sample is standard. See [[Cast iron and carbon equivalent]].
- **Aluminium** — **mill/lathe**, never grind; analyse **immediately** (re-oxidises fast);
  ensure full melting/homogeneity if a re-melt button. See [[Aluminum alloy analysis]].
- **Copper alloys** — mill/lathe; watch **leaded** grades (Pb segregates) → use single-spark
  averaging. See [[Copper alloy analysis]].
- **Nickel / cobalt superalloys** — grind; demanding on resolution and CRMs. See
  [[Nickel cobalt and superalloys]].

## Heat & overheating
Thin, small, or poorly-clamped samples overheat → composition shifts and poor seal. If the part
is unavoidably small, use the correct **adapter** or **re-melt** it
([[Small samples, chips and remelting]]).
