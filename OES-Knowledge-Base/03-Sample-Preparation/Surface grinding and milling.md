# Surface grinding and milling

How to physically produce the clean, flat face from [[Sample preparation overview]].

## Grinding (steel, cast iron, nickel, hard alloys)

- Use a **belt or disc grinder** with **coarse abrasive, ~60–80 grit**. Coarse is correct: it
  cuts fresh metal fast and leaves a uniform, fissured surface the spark likes.
- **Zirconia (zirconia-alumina)** belts are the workhorse for steel; they cut cool and last.
- **Move the sample** / use light, even pressure — don't dwell, which **overheats** the spot and
  can alter composition (carbon loss, retained-austenite changes, smearing).
- Grind **enough to remove all oxide/decarburised skin**, exposing bright metal across the whole
  area that will seal the [[Spark stand and counter electrode|aperture]].

## Milling / facing (aluminium, copper, magnesium, soft metals)

- **Do not grind** soft metals: grinding **smears** them and can **embed abrasive grit** →
  silicon/aluminium contamination and a non-representative surface.
- Use a **milling machine, lathe or fly-cutter** to take a clean cut, leaving a smooth, bright
  face. A fresh cutter and the right feed avoid tearing.

## Avoiding cross-contamination

- **Dedicate abrasives per matrix.** A belt used on a leaded brass will contaminate the next
  steel. Keep separate belts/cutters for steel, aluminium, copper, etc.
- A worn/clogged belt smears rather than cuts — replace on schedule.
- Don't grind right next to other metals' dust; keep the area clean.

## Abrasive quick guide

| Matrix | Tool | Abrasive / grit |
|---|---|---|
| Carbon/low-alloy & stainless steel | Belt/disc grinder | Zirconia 60–80 |
| Cast iron | Belt grinder + **chilled sample** | Zirconia 60–80 |
| Nickel/cobalt alloys | Belt/disc grinder | Zirconia/alumina 60–80 |
| Aluminium | Mill / lathe | Carbide cutter (no abrasive) |
| Copper / brass / bronze | Mill / lathe | Carbide cutter |
