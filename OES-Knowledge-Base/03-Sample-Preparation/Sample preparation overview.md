# Sample preparation overview

> [!important] Sample preparation is the single biggest source of error in OES analysis.

Spark OES analyses **only the thin layer of metal vaporised by the spark**. If that surface is
contaminated, oxidised, or uneven, the reading will be wrong — *no matter how good the
instrument is*. Good preparation produces a **clean, flat, representative metal surface** the
spark can sample consistently.

## The non-negotiables

1. **Clean** — free of oxide scale, oil, paint, plating, rust, cutting fluid, marker.
2. **Flat** — must seal the [[Spark stand and counter electrode|spark-stand aperture]] so no air
   leaks into the [[Argon supply and purity|argon]] atmosphere.
3. **Representative** — the prepared face must reflect the bulk, not a surface-enriched or
   segregated skin (matters for cast iron and weld zones).
4. **Fresh** — analyse soon after preparation; freshly ground metal re-oxidises in minutes,
   especially aluminium and steel.
5. **Cool** — avoid overheating during grinding, which can alter surface composition (e.g. burn
   off carbon, smear soft metals).

## Method by matrix (summary)

- **Steel, cast iron, nickel:** **grind** with coarse abrasive belt/disc (typically **60–80
  grit**, zirconia/alumina) to expose fresh metal.
- **Aluminium, copper, soft metals:** **mill or lathe** a clean cut rather than grind — grinding
  smears the soft metal and can embed abrasive grit. See [[Sample requirements by matrix]].
- **Cast iron carbon:** requires a **chilled (white) sample** for valid carbon — see
  [[Cast iron and carbon equivalent]].

## Common mistakes that ruin results
- Re-using a clogged/contaminated grinding belt across matrices (cross-contamination).
- Grinding too lightly (oxide remains) or too hot (composition changes).
- Sample too small to seal the aperture → air leak → bad UV elements.
- Analysing an old, re-oxidised surface.
- Fingerprints/oil from handling the prepared face.
