# Small samples, chips and remelting

What to do when the part isn't a convenient solid block.

## Small / thin parts → adapters

Spark OES needs the sample to **seal the aperture** and act as a **heat sink**
([[Sample requirements by matrix]]). For wires, thin sheet, small castings or fasteners:

- Use a **reducing adapter / mask** with a smaller spark opening matched to the part.
- For thin sheet, **stack** pieces or back them so they don't overheat/perforate.
- Accept that very small parts give **fewer burn spots** and somewhat worse precision.

## Chips, turnings, drillings, granules → remelting

Loose material cannot be sparked directly. Convert it to a solid disc:

- **Re-melt** the chips into a button/disc in an **arc or induction melter** under inert gas,
  then grind/mill the cast face. Common for foundry returns, machining swarf, and lab samples.
- Ensure **full melting and mixing** for a homogeneous button; slag/oxide must be removed.
- Be aware re-melting can **lose volatile/reactive elements** (e.g. some Mg, Zn, Pb, or C/S
  changes) — for those, prefer a directly-cast or chilled sample, or confirm with
  [[Carbon-sulfur combustion analysis|combustion]] / [[ONH inert gas fusion analysis|fusion]].

## Cast iron special case
For carbon by OES, cast a **chilled (white) sample** so carbon is in solution, not graphite —
see [[Cast iron and carbon equivalent]]. A coin from a chill mould or thermal-analysis cup is
typical.

## When OES isn't the right tool for the sample
- True bulk **C/S** on powders/chips → [[Carbon-sulfur combustion analysis]].
- **O/N/H** → [[ONH inert gas fusion analysis]].
- Non-destructive / no prep possible → [[OES vs XRF vs LIBS|XRF or LIBS]].
