# Cast iron and carbon equivalent

Cast iron is high-carbon (≈2–4 % C) iron with silicon, and its properties hinge on **carbon and
silicon** — making accurate **C** and **Si**, and the derived **carbon equivalent**, the central
task.

## The carbon equivalent (CE / CEV)

The most common foundry formula:

$$CE = \%C + \tfrac{1}{3}(\%Si + \%P)$$

CE locates the iron relative to the **eutectic (~4.3 %)**: below = *hypoeutectic*, above =
*hypereutectic*. It predicts solidification behaviour, fluidity, shrinkage and **chill
tendency**, so foundries adjust the melt to hit a target CE before pouring (see
[[Foundry melt control workflow]]).

## The carbon-by-OES problem — use a CHILLED sample

> [!warning] In grey/ductile iron, much carbon is present as **graphite**, which sparks
> non-uniformly and reads low/erratically. **You cannot get valid carbon by sparking a normal
> grey-iron casting.**

The standard fix: cast a **chilled (white-iron) sample**. Rapid cooling (a chill mould or a
thermal-analysis cup with a chill) keeps the carbon **in solution / as carbide** rather than
graphite, so the spark sees it uniformly. Grind the chilled face and analyse it
([[Small samples, chips and remelting]], [[Sample requirements by matrix]]).

- **OES (chilled sample)** is fast and gives full chemistry (C, Si, Mn, P, S, Mg, Cr, Cu, Sn …).
- **Combustion C/S** ([[Carbon-sulfur combustion analysis]]) is the **reference for total carbon
  and sulfur** and is matrix-independent — many foundries use OES for the full element panel and
  combustion to confirm C/S.
- **Thermal analysis** (cooling-curve cups) gives CE/C/Si very fast at the furnace but is less
  precise than OES/combustion.

## Key elements & why
- **C, Si** → CE, graphitisation, strength. **Mn, S** → matrix/pearlite, MnS. **Mg, Ce** →
  nodularity in **ductile** iron. **Cr, Cu, Sn, Mo** → pearlite/alloying. **P** → fluidity/
  brittleness.
