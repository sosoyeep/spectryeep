# Spark and arc excitation

The **excitation** step delivers energy to the sample, vaporising and exciting its atoms. Two
classical modes exist; modern instruments blend and digitally control them.

## Spark vs arc

| | **Spark** | **Arc / DC arc** |
|---|---|---|
| Discharge | Repetitive high-voltage breakdowns | Continuous low-voltage current |
| Sampling | Small spot, many discharges, very repeatable | Larger, hotter, more material |
| Strength | **Best precision**, alloy quantification | Higher sensitivity for trace work |
| Use today | Standard for solid-metal quantitative OES | Niche / legacy |

Most metals-industry OES today is **spark** excitation, generated by a controllable
[[Excitation source (spark generator)|digital spark source]].

## Anatomy of a measurement

1. **Pre-spark (pre-burn / pre-integration).** An initial series of discharges cleans and
   homogenises the spot — burning off the ground layer, oxides and surface contamination so the
   analysed plasma reflects the bulk metal. Skipping or shortening pre-spark is a classic cause
   of biased results.
2. **Integration (measurement).** Once the spot stabilises, the instrument integrates emission
   over many discharges to average out shot-to-shot variation.
3. **Multiple sparks averaged.** Typical analyses fire **hundreds of discharges per second**
   and average them; a full burn is a few seconds.

## Parameters the source controls
- Discharge **energy** and **frequency** (higher energy → more ablation, more sensitivity, but
  more heating).
- **Waveform / mode** tuned per matrix (unipolar vs bipolar, capacitance, inductance).
- These are encoded in **excitation parameter sets** that ship with each calibration program.

## Single-spark information
Individual discharges carry information about **homogeneity**: a stream of single-spark
intensities reveals inclusions and segregation. See [[Single spark evaluation (PDA-SDA)]].
