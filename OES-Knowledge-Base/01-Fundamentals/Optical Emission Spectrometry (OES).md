# Optical Emission Spectrometry (OES)

**Optical emission spectrometry (OES)** — also called *spark atomic emission spectrometry
(spark-AES)* or, in industry shorthand, the *spark spectrometer* — is one of the most widely
used techniques for determining the **elemental composition of metals**. From incoming raw-
material checks to final certification, it gives foundries, steel mills and metal fabricators
fast, accurate readings of dozens of elements at once.

## Principle in one paragraph

An electrical discharge is created between an electrode and the metal sample. The energy
vaporises a microscopic amount of material and excites the atoms, which emit light at
wavelengths characteristic of each element (see [[Atomic emission and spectral lines]]). By
measuring the **intensity** of these wavelengths, the spectrometer calculates the
**concentration** of each element present — calibrated against certified reference materials.

## What makes it distinctive

- **Measures the light elements.** Carbon, phosphorus, sulfur, boron and nitrogen — exactly
  the elements that define steel and cast-iron grades — are read directly. This is the headline
  advantage over [[OES vs XRF vs LIBS|XRF]].
- **Multi-element & simultaneous.** 20 to 70+ elements in a single burn of a few seconds.
- **Low detection limits.** Down to single-digit **ppm** for many elements with a good
  vacuum/UV optical path.
- **Solid samples, minimal consumables.** No acids or sample digestion; argon, electrodes and
  abrasive are the main consumables (see [[Total cost of ownership and consumables]]).

## Where it sits among related techniques

| Need | Best fit |
|---|---|
| Carbon & low element ppm in a lab, certification | **Spark OES** |
| Fast, non-destructive sorting of heavier elements | [[OES vs XRF vs LIBS\|XRF]] |
| Handheld carbon-equivalent sorting in the field | [[OES vs XRF vs LIBS\|LIBS]] |
| C/S at high precision / wide range | [[Carbon-sulfur combustion analysis]] |
| O/N/H gases in metal | [[ONH inert gas fusion analysis]] |
